\section{Idea}

HSIC gives a \emph{symmetric association} between two metrics. But to understand
whether the \emph{relationship} between a target metric $Y$ (say GMV) and the
other metrics $X$ has \emph{changed} between an existing and a new batch, we need
a target-oriented, conditional object, not a pairwise one. We use the
pseudo-outcome (PO-)risk with the batch label $W$ as a ``treatment'': it detects
a change in $P(Y\mid X)$ (concept drift), and a leave-one-out (LOCO) tells us
\emph{which} $X$ the change loads on. This is \textbf{one model per target $Y$}
--- the learner takes all of $X$ jointly --- so it costs $O(\#\text{targets})$
models, not $O(p^2)$ models for every $X$--$Y$ pair.

\section{PO-risk}

With cross-fitted nuisances $\mu(x)=\E[Y\mid x]$ and $e(x)=\Pr(W{=}1\mid x)$,
form the pseudo-outcome $\psi=(Y-\mu(X))\,(W-e(X))$ and fit
$\tau=\argmin_f \E[(\psi-f(X))^2]$. The statistic is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PO\text{-}risk}=\E\big[\tau(X)^2\big],
\end{equation}
which is large when $\tau(X)=\E[Y\mid X,W{=}1]-\E[Y\mid X,W{=}0]\neq0$, i.e.\ when
$P(Y\mid X)$ differs across batches, and $\approx 0$ under pure covariate shift.
A permute-then-refit $p$-value (shuffle $W$) tests significance.

\paragraph{LOCO.} The importance of metric $x_j$ is the drop in PO-risk when it
is removed, $\phi_j=\mathrm{PO\text{-}risk}(X)-\mathrm{PO\text{-}risk}(X_{-j})$:
removing the metric that carries the $Y\mid X$ change collapses the detected
drift.

\section{Method}

Choose a target $Y$; let $X$ be the other metrics and $W$ the batch. (1) Run the
PO-risk permutation test to decide whether $P(Y\mid X)$ shifted. (2) If so, rank
the $X$ by LOCO to localize where the change sits. One fit of the meta-learner
covers all $X$ jointly.

\section{Result}

Synthetic metrics are drawn i.i.d.\ (no covariate shift); the target $Y=$ GMV
keeps a stable mapping but, \emph{for the new batch only}, gains a dependence on
\texttt{rating} (the rating\,$\to$\,GMV relationship strengthens) --- a concept
drift localized to \texttt{rating}.

\paragraph{Takeaway.} For a target metric, PO-risk answers the question HSIC
cannot --- \emph{did $Y\mid X$ change, and on which $X$} --- with a single
meta-learner per target and a LOCO read-out, avoiding an $O(p^2)$ battery of
pairwise models.
